# Tutorial 8: Life Scenes. Your recording

**What to record:** your voice only, reading the text below. No screen, no camera.
**Length:** about 367 words, roughly 2.4 minutes at a relaxed pace.

## How to record
- A quiet room, your normal speaking pace, one take you like. Mistakes are fine: say the line again and keep going. I cut the repeats.
- Where you see (short pause), take a breath. Do not read the words in parentheses.
- Leave about one second of silence before the first word and after the last.
- Save it as WAV, MP3 or M4A. Send it here in the chat. If it is too big, share a link and set sharing to "Anyone with the link can view", then back to Restricted when I confirm I have it.
- Change any word that does not sound like you. Tell me what you changed so the on-screen words match.

## What I need from you before I build
- **Two or three short clips** for the examples (Google Flow, Veo, Seedance, Kling, stock or your own camera). If a clip has music or speech baked in, tell me. If you have none handy, say so and I will use what is already in the project.
- **Your approval of the on-screen words.** I will write them first (about 22 characters a line, two or three lines a card, generic names only) and send them to you before I build anything.

## What I build from your recording
Three finished Life Scenes (a text-message overlay, a note, a notification stack) and a short clip of each camera move: zoom, pullback, punch, drift and freeze.

## Read this

Hi, it's Dr. CK. A Life Scene is a short cinematic clip with a message on it. It has no talking head. The words on the screen are the point. They turn a pretty clip into something people remember.

Where do the clips come from? Google Flow, Veo, Seedance or Kling, stock footage, or your own camera. If a clip has its own music or speech baked in, listen to it first.

The command is: Make a Life Scene. Then you tell it a few things. A title. The clip or clips. The message of the scene. And whether you have a voiceover.

Here's what I like most about how it works. The assistant writes the on-screen words first, and you approve them before anything is built. The lines stay short, about 22 characters each, two or three lines on a card. They're written the way you talk, so they make sense on a single hearing. If you give it stiff wording from a prompt, it rewrites it in plain English.

Then the camera. Each clip gets one move. A slow zoom pushes in. A pullback starts on a detail and reveals the scene. A punch is a quick zoom on a beat. A drift is a gentle handheld sway. And a freeze holds the frame while your text lands. (short pause)

Now the overlays, which are my favorite part. A text message that types out, sends, and gets a reply. A paper note that writes itself line by line. A stack of notification cards. A tilted phone with a list of recordings. A checklist that ticks off, one item at a time. Comments that pop in with a heart. And a timeline that lights up one point at a time. (short pause)

Every word inside those overlays comes from you. The names and handles stay generic, never a real person's. That's on purpose.

Life Scenes are vertical, 1080 by 1920. Before it renders, the assistant pulls a frame at every text moment so you can check that nothing is cut off and nothing sits on a face. You approve, and it renders.

Use a Life Scene between your talking points, or on its own. One clip, one message, one feeling.
