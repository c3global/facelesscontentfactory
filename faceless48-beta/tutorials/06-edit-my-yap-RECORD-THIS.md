# Tutorial 6: Edit my yap. Your recording

**What to record:** your voice only, reading the text below. No screen, no camera.
**Length:** about 340 words, roughly 2.3 minutes at a relaxed pace.

## How to record
- A quiet room, your normal speaking pace, one take you like. Mistakes are fine: say the line again and keep going. I cut the repeats.
- Where you see (short pause), take a breath. Do not read the words in parentheses.
- Leave about one second of silence before the first word and after the last.
- Save it as WAV, MP3 or M4A. Send it here in the chat. If it is too big, share a link and set sharing to "Anyone with the link can view", then back to Restricted when I confirm I have it.
- Change any word that does not sound like you. Tell me what you changed so the on-screen words match.

## What I need from you before I build
- **One raw, unedited yap** (a real one you recorded in the car or at home, original audio, nothing run through a voice changer). I use it for the before and after.
- Tell me if there is a name or link said in it that I should check.

## What I build from your recording
The raw clip, then the finished one after "Edit my yap", plus a short clip of the command being typed.

## Read this

Hi, it's Dr. CK. A yap is a casual talking video. In the car. On the couch. At your desk. It's supposed to feel real, so the edit is light on purpose.

Here's the raw clip. (short pause) Now here's the same clip after one command.

The command is: Edit my yap.

What did it do? It added word-by-word captions, with a few key words in color. It trimmed the long pauses. It added a quiet music bed. It put in two or three slow zooms. And it left the room showing, so you still feel like you're in the car.

Let's talk about the pauses. The plug-in tightens every silence longer than a set length. The default is three tenths of a second. If you want looser, ask for four tenths. If you want it snappier, ask for two tenths. (short pause) These are jump cuts, so the assistant checks your pose at each cut.

Before it edits, it shows you the transcript and asks you to confirm the words. Check your name and any links.

If you recorded your yap in a few pieces, you can say: Join these clips into one yap. It lines them up, then edits the joined video.

What a yap does not get: no end card, and it ends right after your last line, before your hand reaches for the camera. No stack of effects. A yap should look like a yap, not like a polished talking head.

Keep your original audio. Voice-changing tools can strip out the room sound, and the room sound is what makes a yap feel real.

If it ever says "that's a talking head, not a yap," it will ask you one time which you mean. Pick one, and it keeps going.

A quick note on captions. The captions can look a hair late against a mouth, especially on an AI avatar. If you see a caption that's off, tell the assistant the exact second and the word. That's how it gets fixed.

That's a yap. One command, light edit, real feel.
