# Tutorial 7: Animated explainers. Your recording

**What to record:** your voice only, reading the text below. No screen, no camera.
**Length:** about 363 words, roughly 6 to 7 minutes at a relaxed pace.

## How to record
- A quiet room, your normal speaking pace, one take you like. Mistakes are fine: say the line again and keep going. I cut the repeats.
- Leave about one second of silence before the first word and after the last.
- Save it as WAV, MP3 or M4A. Send it here in the chat. If it is too big, share a link and set sharing to "Anyone with the link can view", then back to Restricted when I confirm I have it.
- Change any word that does not sound like you. Tell me what you changed so the on-screen words match.

## One thing I need from you
The "Load versus Capacity" layout shows how much something carries against what it can carry. It needs two **real** numbers from you, and I will not invent them. Pick any pair that is true for you, for example hours of editing you used to spend per video against hours you want to spend. Send me the two numbers and what they measure.

## Read this

Hi, it's Dr. CK. Here's how you turn your voice into an animated video, with no camera and nothing to film.

All you need is a voiceover. Your own recording. Say this: Turn my voiceover into an explainer.

The recording is what everything is timed to. The plug-in reads the words you said and places each picture on the exact word. A written script by itself can't do that, which is why we start from audio.

Now the look. There are three styles. Whiteboard is clean line art. Editorial is led by serif typography. Dark is a dark background with light figures. Before it builds the whole video, it shows you a still of the style so you can say yes or no.

Next, layouts. These are the ways a scene can be organized, and your assistant picks the one that fits your idea. Compare puts two things side by side. Numbered List walks through steps. Layers stacks ideas. Load versus Capacity shows how much something is carrying against what it can carry. Process or Loop shows a cycle.

A heads-up on that one. Load versus Capacity needs real numbers from you. The assistant won't invent them. If you don't give it numbers, it picks another layout.

Who is on screen? By default, a stick figure drawn in code, so you need nothing. If you have your own character art, a 2D or 3D character you own, you can use that instead. Give it clear poses, all the same size, on a transparent background, and it builds around them. If you use a character, make sure you own the rights.

You can use portrait for Reels, TikTok and Shorts, or landscape for YouTube. Here's the same explainer in both.

You can also make a video out of your own images and footage with a voiceover. Same command, same process. You bring the pieces, it builds the timing.

And just like your talking heads, you can change anything with a sentence. Make the figure smaller. Use the dark style. Change the third scene to a list.

Every explainer is a little lesson, and the examples you're seeing are the lesson. Make one this week.
